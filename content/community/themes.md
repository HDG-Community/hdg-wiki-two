Fiction has themes. Even if you're not trying to (or even if you're aggressively trying not to), when you put pen to paper to create a narrative you are touching on the things that inform the story, inform how you the writer are interacting with the story, and how the reader will interact with the story. This is true even if you're just writing smut. 

These are some of the core themes of Human Domestication Guide as a setting. 
### Disability
Human Domestication Guide is a disability narrative. This is woven deep into the fabric of the setting, so deeply that it's easy to miss if you're not clued in. Everything about HDG is asking the question: what if all the uncaring authority figures who act out of what they claim are your own best interests _actually cared?_ What if they not only _did_ have your best interests at heart, but had the means and will to follow through on it, to make sure _no one slips through the cracks_, and that everyone is cared for?

Let's take a simple example: stories are fond of showcasing the Affini building public infrastructure that's sized for them rather than for the significantly smaller humans around them. Textually, this is about Affini condescension, normalizing dependency, and so on. Metatextually, however, _this is a fantasy of everyone, not just disabled folks, needing help to do basic everything things._

A lot of what comes across as weirdly passive-aggressive behavior by the Affini, the way they condescend, the way they touch uninvited, the way they invite themselves into the lives of others — is part of this narrative. Disabled folks are commonly infantilized by others — in professional settings like medical care, interpersonal settings, governmental & political settings, etc — so once the Affini show up and start running things according to their own internal sense of ethics and morality _everyone_ gets the same treatment.

Once you see it through this lens, it's pretty hard to unsee. Take a minute, and get your head around it.

The reason the Affini are the way they are is because, through the world they create in the fiction, they allow us as writers and readers to figuratively inhabit a world where we're not marked as a class by our disabilities, because the Affini place us all in the same class by comparison. Even if we're not writing about this specifically, we're writing about it because it's part of the themes baked directly into the setting, the themes that set the Affini inextricably above all other life in the universe.

I'll say it again, for emphasis: *HDG is a disability fantasy where being disabled doesn't matter, because the Affini treat everyone the way disabled people are treated.* If you want to write in the setting, you must internalize this understanding of the setting — even, and especially, if you're not disabled yourself. 
### Asexuality and Queerness 
There are lots of bodice-rippers out there about the horny, rapacious alien empire having their way with the defenseless (often white) human women at the center of the story. Like most bodice-rippers, these are fantasies of powerlessness meant to facilitate a sexual domination that is desired but also taboo. Through these stories, the reader experiences their desire without the shame or guilt of having had to voice it, because the character they're intended to identify with never had a choice. 

This is not, generally speaking, what HDG is doing.

HDG is, in some ways, a setting for bodice-rippers, but not always the sort described above. The Affini, as originally designed, _do not necessarily have what we would recognize as a sex drive_. They do not generally take florets to fuck them, or even to use them in whatever vestigial natural reproductive drive they might not yet have engineered out of themselves. The Affini take florets because they see it as their duty to care for the universe, and for every living sophont in it. It scratches a "cuteness aggression" itch, perhaps, and they take great joy in it, but it not an impulse that humans would recognize as sexual.  

This does not mean that the Affini _cannot_ have sex. They absolutely can, especially if it's something that's vital for the well-being of their pets. Additionally, when the Affini engage in things we would mark as sex (even the broader categories of sex, such as BDSM), to the Affini, it's not always _sex_ as we'd identify it: it's more like breaking out the laser pointer for the cat to chase. It's _fun_. It's stimulation for the pet and enjoyment for the owner getting to watch their pet go nuts trying to catch the Nefarious Red Dot. 

Can the Affini give themselves grafts to facilitate a more traditional sex act with a floret? Sure. Can the Affini even engineer a traditional Terran sex drive into themselves? Yeah, that's well within their ability. It's just an intentional choice by that Affini. Sexual desire is not necessarily how the Affini as a group engages with sex, even when when they ravish their floret in a way said floret absolutely experiences as sexual.

Sidenote for clarity: Allosexual (non-Asexual) folks are absolutely welcome in the community, and welcome to contribute, and even welcome to write about their own experiences. Of course one can reasonably expect that with many humans being allosexual, it's not unusual for sexuality to be one of the many levers that Affini might use to engage with their pets and ensure they'll be happy and fulfilled. But it shouldn't be assumed by writers or readers that all Affini-floret relationships are sexual, or necessarily even romantic, though they are all deeply committed and intimate and, on a structural level, kinky.

Beyond asexuality, HDG is also a very, _very_ queer setting. You may have noticed the prevalence of transgender protagonists, a good many of whom are also gay. This is not accidental either; most of the HDG community, and virtually all of the original writers upon whom the community's work is built, are trans and queer. Asexuality is particularly centered, but we celebrate queerness of all kinds here. There is no rule against m/m or even m/f pairings; it's just that most of the community are somewhere in the general vicinity of trans lesbians in terms of their gender and orientation. 
### Neurodiversity 
HDG protagonists are frequently neurodivergent, being autistic, having ADHD, BPD, or any number of other traits that are marked as medical conditions by our modern medical infrastructure. Neurodiverse folks are often treated as problems to be solved to better adapt us to a social model of productivity and profit, rather than being given the tools and aid we need to thrive on our own terms. 

Obviously, this is not the case in the Affini Compact. The Affini do not tolerate capitalism, scarcity, or exploitation, and consequently have no motive to cram a square peg into a round hole. Neurodiversity is treated as something to be not just accommodated, but celebrated. This isn't just wish fulfillment, but a deep and abiding worldview given sanction by the universalist message of the Affini and their great common task: every sophont is worth caring for, and care can mean very different things from individual to individual. 

On a purely Doyleist level, of course, HDG is overwhelmingly written by neurodiverse creators. We write what we know, from our own perspectives. Consequently, a great many of our characters inherit our neurodiverse traits, including many affini. We center neurodiverse narratives in HDG because it speaks to who we are. There are probably neurotypical (or at least, inasmuch as neurotypical folks exist as opposed to folks closer to the "ideal" on the spectrum are able to fake it convincingly and without strain) folks in the community, certainly in the readership if not overwhelmingly on the creative side, and they are absolutely welcome in the community, but much like with asexuality, we do ask that those who don't consider themselves to be part of the neurodiverse community remember whose space they're in when picking up the pen to write. 
### Utopianism 
There's no child in the basement. Sorry. Wrong story.

To be a little less glib about it: Yes, the Affini are an empire, endlessly expanding, forcibly integrating species they encounter into their all-consuming quest to care for all sophont life. No, they are not doing it with any ulterior motive in mind. When they say "we're here to help," they mean it. They just also don't take no for an answer. 

This is a corollary theme to Disability, above. The Affini aren't willing to accept "no, we're fine, thanks." Everyone gets to be a part of their utopia, and it is a utopia. There's no seamy underbelly to it; the closest the Compact gets to dirty secrets are cotyledons — the forced medical experiments that serve as a prelude to every domestication campaign — and even then, they don't hide cotyledons away and pretend they don't exist, _they celebrate them._

The Affini aren't bullshitting. There's no nefarious secret plan. There's no grand goal beyond the one they'll happily infodump to you about. They're here to help, and they won't take no for an answer. _The horror is the comfort, and the comfort is the horror._
