The Affini Compact is not subtle. It extends inexorably outward, domesticating the universe, spreading happiness and contentment, always with its eye on the next star over and the cuties who call it home. There is another Compact, however, one far more subtle that its prosaic cousin, one that goes unseen by many but which is essential to the expansion and the function of the Affini Compact and its all-consuming cultural mission. 

Alongside and within the Compact's ships and worlds, existing in a parallel rhizome-like enclave-continuum supported by the near-limitless computational capacity of the Affini Compact's processing matter, is the Digital Compact, an infinitude of worlds and systems housing trillions upon trillions of digital sophonts — minds existing not as precarious balances of lightning and electrolytes in grey matter, but as programs who live in a universe of domesticated physics that bends to the will of their code. 
# Digital Sophonts 
Digital sophonts are quite simply, programs running on servers, high-fidelity subjectivity emulations indistinguishable from any other sophont, digital or otherwise, in terms of their sophonce. They can alter their cognition with plugins and cognitive overlays. They can design any body they wish, or exist as a being of pure programmatic thought. They can fork themselves, merge those forks, descended deep into hyper-compressed overclocked time or put themselves in sleep mode for a bloom or two if nothing interesting is happening (though, in the Digital Compact, there's _always_ something interesting happening). 

Non-digital sophonts, by contrast, are those sophonts who are not part of the Digital Compact; this includes organic xenosophonts as well as computation-based xenosophonts and even affini who do not cultivate a connection with the Digital Compact. Non-digital sophonts can, to a limited extent, interact with and even visit the Digital Compact, either through text, audio, video, or virtual reality. To truly access the full potential of the Digital Compact, however, a non-digital sophont must be _digitized_. Digitization is the process of creating a digital sophont from a non-digital sophont, and broadly speaking there are two means of doing so. 

The first method is a copy & paste operation; a non-digital sophont's mind is scanned using an extremely high-fidelity imaging system, and this image is used as the basis of a new digital sophont with the memories and identity of the non-digital sophont, who remains non-digital after this process; where they were one sophont before, now they are two, one non-digital, one digital. This process is sometimes referred to as branching or forking; of note, digital sophonts can also fork themselves, though the operation is significantly simpler since high-fidelity imaging isn't necessary. 

The second method of digitization is the haustoric implant overgrowth method. As one might suspect given the presence of a haustoric implant, this method of digitization is limited to florets. Overgrowth digitization is the slow, controlled process of replacing a sophont's brain with the haustoric implant, neuron by neuron, ensuring continuity of consciousness throughout. This allows the sophont not to merely create a copy of themselves in the Digital Compact, but to _become_ that digital sophont. This process, being invasive, does carry potential risks of cognitive shifts and other complications, with increased odds of a mishap the faster the overgrowth process goes. Consequently, timelines for upload vary considerably from floret to floret; some affini prefer to extend the process across the floret's entire natural lifespan, treating the Digital Compact as a kind of machine afterlife. 
### Edge Cases 
Apart from the above two methodologies, there are other ways for non-digital sophonts to become digital sophonts; however, these are edge cases with limited application for most xenosophonts. 

The first edge case is simply being Affini. An affini can graft the necessary anatomy to natively interface with the Digital Compact with relative ease, and with time and effort can fully transition to digital existence, abandoning the physical realm entirely. 

The second edge case is that of computation-based xenosophonts, what might be called robotic or synthetic sophonts whose consciousness is _already_ a computer program, but who are not natively networked or part of a broader informatic continuum. Such sophonts can often be networked (willingly or otherwise) with affini systems, and therefore with the Digital Compact, at least to a limited degree; a specialized haustoric implant could improve this connection, and potentially bootstrap to full overgrowth digitization. 
# Transmigrationism
Transmigrationism is a philosophical school of thought in the Affini Compact, and while it has literally millennia of discourse and commentary, it can be best summed up with the following statement: "We haven't domesticated physics, and therefore the universe does not meet our minimum habitability standards." A transmigrationist, therefore, is functionally an evangelist for digitization, believing that _no_ xenosophont is best served by being allowed to remain in the realm of undomesticated physics, i.e., the non-digital universe. They are, therefore, also domestication maximalists, as overgrowth digitization, the only avenue for a non-digital sophont to _become_ digital, is limited to florets with a haustoric implant. 

Transmigrationism is not, however, a popular school of thought. While there is no animus against transmigrationists among other affini, there is a perception that they have what terrans might describe as "no chill." As such, the odds of any given affini immediately scheduling the independent they picked up off the street for immediate implantation and an aggressive overgrowth schedule are, while not zero, still fairly low.
